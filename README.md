### The Problem Statement - Cascading Failure: When One Failure Becomes Many
> Infrastructure is often monitored asset by asset, even though roads, bridges, hospitals, utilities, and other critical systems depend on one another. A failure at one location can change traffic or resource flows, overload alternative routes or services, and create consequences far beyond the original point of failure. Understanding resilience therefore requires looking at infrastructure as an interconnected system rather than as a collection of independent assets. Design a system that represents a selected infrastructure network and explores how disruptions could propagate through it. The system should allow users to examine relationships, introduce one or more failures, estimate wider effects, identify disproportionately important assets or connections, and compare alternative scenarios. Teams are free to define the infrastructure domain, geographic scope, disruption model, impact measures, data sources, and analytical approach. The goal is to help planners understand where intervention could have the greatest effect, while leaving the modeling and system design open to innovation.
